# Content Strategy

This file works alongside brand-voice.md and icp.md. Every piece of content reads all three before writing.



---



## Goal

Grow the client base and increase bookings — treatments and Panchakarma programmes. Instagram is the primary growth channel. Content builds trust first; bookings follow.

**Six-month success metric:** inbound enquiries via email to double from 3–4 per month to 8 per month.



---



## Platform priority

**Instagram — primary.** One post per week, building to two. This is the channel to grow.

**Facebook** — secondary. Existing presence; not the focus for active growth right now.

**Email** — quarterly. Serves existing clients and warm leads. Retention and relationship, not acquisition.

**Website** — passive. Should support discovery and convert visitors who arrive from Instagram or search.



---



## Posting rhythm

Quality beats frequency in this niche. Better to publish one true thing every Tuesday for a year than three things a week for six weeks and burn out. The cadence below reflects that.

**Phase one — months one to three:**
- One feed post per week
- Two stories per week
- One reel per month

**Phase two — month four onwards, once the rhythm feels sustainable:**
- Two feed posts per week — one written for Sarah, one written for Rachel
- Two to three stories per week
- One reel per fortnight

Do not move to phase two until phase one feels effortless. The brand voice is gentle and unhurried. The content cadence should be too.

When stuck: pick a pillar, pick a persona, write one true thing.



---



## The four content pillars

### 1. Treatment and philosophy education — 30%
*What treatments feel like, what they do, and the Ayurvedic principles behind them.*

The philosophy of doshas, gut health, consistency, the bespoke approach. Positions the clinic as a place of genuine knowledge, not just treatments.

**What it earns:** women who are curious but do not yet understand what we offer. This content converts browsers into followers and followers into enquirers.

**Best formats:** carousel, reel, long caption with single image.

**Write for:** Sarah as default. Rachel recognises herself.

*Examples: what Shirodhara actually feels like, why no two Panchakarma programmes look the same, what kapha means and why it matters in spring.*

---

### 2. Condition and healing content — 25%
*Ayurveda's perspective on specific conditions and symptoms.*

Thyroid imbalance, autoimmune, nervous system depletion, burnout, dysautonomia, fatigue that will not shift. Written with care — informative, never prescriptive, never claiming to replace medical advice.

**What it earns:** deep authority with women already on the page or in the funnel. Rachel has been following for weeks or months; this content is what finally makes her email. It is the content doing the longest, quietest work.

**Best formats:** long caption, carousel, reel, quarterly email deep-dive.

**Write for:** Rachel primary. Sarah welcome but not the intended reader.

*Examples: why the nervous system needs rest before any detox, what Ayurveda says about wired-but-tired, how Hashimoto's is approached differently here.*

---

### 3. Clinic, people and story — 25%
*The real place, the real people, the real history.*

Jay's story, Amitha's role, the 20-year legacy, the Maharishi lineage, behind the scenes of a treatment day. This is trust-building content. It answers the question: who are these people and can I trust them with my body?

**What it earns:** trust before the booking — women who have been following for weeks or months finally feel ready to reach out.

**Best formats:** single image with long caption, reel, story.

**Write for:** both Sarah and Rachel equally. Trust is not condition-specific.

*Examples: how Jay found Maharishi Ayurveda, what a pulse diagnosis with Amitha involves, what the clinic looks like on a quiet Tuesday morning.*

---

### 4. Seasonal and lifestyle — 20%
*Ayurveda is seasonal. Each season brings a natural content angle.*

What the body needs at different times of year, which treatments suit the season, when to book and why. Light, accessible, shareable. Does not require prior knowledge of Ayurveda.

**What it earns:** shares and new followers — this is the content women send to friends. It widens the top of the funnel without asking anything of the reader.

**Best formats:** single image with short caption, story, reel.

**Write for:** Sarah primary. Accessible enough that no Ayurvedic knowledge is needed.

*Examples: spring as the ideal time for Panchakarma, what autumn means for vata, why January is not the right time for a harsh detox.*

## Topics never to write about

- Detailed price breakdowns in feed posts — share prices in response to enquiries, not in content
- Hard sales pitches or urgency language ("book now," "limited spaces," "don't miss out")
- Anything a client shared in a private email or consultation — no case studies without explicit permission
- Anything that could read as medical advice or a diagnosis
- Competing clinics, named or implied
- Specific religious claims that narrow the audience — the spiritual register is available, but quiet and open, not doctrinal
- American wellness language — see brand-voice.md for the full avoidance list



---



## Writing rules

- **She stays for the voice, not the information.** Every post, every format.
- In phase one, write for Sarah as the default. Check Rachel recognises herself before posting.
- In phase two, aim for one Sarah post and one Rachel post per week. Posts from the Clinic and story pillar serve both and can fill either slot.
- Hold the medical thread lightly — not every post is about illness.
- British spelling and phrasing always.
- Sign off Jay x.
- **Spiritual register:** use the openly spiritual voice — quiet, reverent, no drama — only when something genuinely moves you. It cannot be manufactured. See Post 4 in brand-voice.md for the benchmark. This may happen a handful of times a year. When it does, trust it.
- Stories do not need to be polished. They need to be real.
- **Hashtags:** strategy to be decided after the first three months of posting. Do not include hashtags in drafts unless explicitly asked.
- Avoid: resonate, manifesting, amazing, transformational, next level, wellness journey, unlock, self care goals, glow up.



---



## Sign-off examples

Never end with a hard call to action. These are the patterns that feel right:

- *"If you are curious whether this might suit you, my inbox is always open. Jay x"*
- *"Have a think, and if you'd like to know more just get in touch — text, call or email anytime. Jay x"*
- *"If any of this sounds familiar, it might be worth a conversation. Jay x"*

**Ayurfest, London — late May 2026**
Three-part content moment:
- *Before:* we are attending, what Ayurfest is, come and find us
- *During:* real-time stories and a feed post — behind the scenes, conversations, energy of the day
- *After:* reflection post — what came up, what people were asking, what it confirmed about the work



---



## Solving the blank page

When there is nothing obvious to post, use this sequence:

1. Pick a pillar from the four above
2. Ask: what is one true thing I know about this that my reader does not?
3. Write it warm, unhurried, specific
4. End with one of the sign-off patterns above — never a hard call to action

One true thing, well said, is always enough.

## What success looks like in six months

- Inbound enquiries via email: 3–4 per month rising to 8 per month
- A consistent, recognisable voice on Instagram that feels like Jay — not a brand, not a template
- A growing follower base of women who match the Sarah and Rachel profiles
- Phase one rhythm (one post, two stories, one reel per month) running without strain
- A clear sense of whether phase two cadence is sustainable before committing to it
